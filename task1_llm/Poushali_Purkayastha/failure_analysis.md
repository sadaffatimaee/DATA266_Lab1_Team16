Three failure cases from the samples in outputs/full/samples.txt, generated by checkpoints/full/final.pt (run full_20260928_180129, RTX 5090). Each snippet is copied verbatim from the samples file.

## Case 1: repetition loop under greedy decoding

- Prompt and decoding: "One day, a little girl", greedy (sample 6)
- Generated snippet: `She said, "Lily, you can have a ball too. It is my ball. It is my ball. It is my ball. It is my ball. It is my ball. It is my ball. It has a ball. It can make a ball. It can make a ball. It is a ball. It can make a ball. It can make a ball. It is a ball.`
- Failure type: repetition
- Observation: greedy decoding always takes the most likely next character, and once the model has produced "It is my ball." twice, that phrase is the highest-probability continuation of itself, so the model never leaves the loop. The same mechanism shows in sample 1 and sample 11, where greedy decoding re-starts the dataset's most common opening ("Once upon a time, there was a little girl named Lily. She loved to play outside in the sunshine") after the first paragraph. None of the temperature samples loop this way, and the repeated 4-gram rate across all samples is only 5%, so the problem is the decoder, not the model's knowledge. A repetition penalty or nucleus sampling would remove it at no training cost.

## Case 2: invented and truncated words at temperature 1.0

- Prompt and decoding: "The cat", temperature 1.0 (sample 14)
- Generated snippet: `He wanted to unlect his ordinary too.` and later `Maybe he wanted to do it, so his mom roared and his car cra. We could say fast!`
- Failure type: broken grammar and hallucinated tokens
- Observation: a character-level model has no word vocabulary, so at high temperature it can sample a plausible-looking letter sequence that is not a word ("unlect") or cut a word short when a sentence-ending character is sampled early ("cra."). Both happen only at temperature 1.0; the eight samples at greedy and temperature 0.7 contain no non-words. Distinct-1 is higher at temperature 1.0 for the same reason, so the diversity metrics and this failure are two views of the same sampling noise. Lowering the temperature or applying top-k sampling trades a little diversity for well-formed words.

## Case 3: self-contradicting narrative

- Prompt and decoding: "One day, a little girl", temperature 0.7 (sample 8)
- Generated snippet: `One day, a little girl named Lily went outside and saw a little girl named Lily. She loved to play with her toys and dress up in her hands. One day, she went to the park with her mom and dad. They had many ducks and tasted on the road.` and later `Mom said they lost their mom and dad.`
- Failure type: loss of coherence
- Observation: every sentence is grammatical and every phrase is a common TinyStories phrase, but the story contradicts itself: Lily meets a second Lily, a mother reports that they lost their mother, and "tasted on the road" pairs a verb with the wrong object. The model's 128-character context covers about two sentences, so it cannot track who the characters are across a paragraph and stitches locally likely fragments together. Sample 3 shows the same thing with "a big hill who wanted to fly it". A longer context or a word-level tokenizer would let the model carry entities further; within this architecture, more training data would help less than more context.

## Summary

All three failures are consistent with the metrics: the model predicts the next character well (76% top-1, 1.08 bits per character) but has no memory beyond 128 characters and no notion of words. Cases 1 and 2 are decoding choices and fixable at inference time; case 3 is an architectural limit of the context length.
